# Intensity & zones

Use the athlete's own 5-zone heart-rate model given in the context (never assume another one).

## Mapping to the 3-zone (Seiler) model
- Low intensity = Z1 + Z2 (below the first threshold, conversational).
- Middle / "grey zone" = Z3 (tempo; useful in moderation, easy to overdo).
- High intensity = Z4 + Z5 (threshold and above).

## Intensity distribution
- Endurance athletes improve most with roughly 80% of training time at low intensity and ~20% high (polarized). A pyramidal week (lots of low, some middle, little high) is also effective, especially in base phases.
- Polarization index = (%low + %high) / %middle. Above 2.0 = well polarized; 1.5–2.0 = moderately; below 1.5 = threshold-heavy.
- Warning sign: most rides drifting into Z3 "because it feels productive" — this builds fatigue without the benefit of true easy or true hard work.

## Judging a session against its intent
- Steady Z2 ride: success if ≥70% of moving time sits in Z2 (Z1 time is fine), Z4+ under ~5%.
- Threshold / intervals: look for repeatable work bouts (similar HR peaks across reps, not climbing each rep) and real recovery between them.
- Recovery ride: almost all time in Z1, short and easy.
- Heart rate lags effort by 1–3 minutes; short climbs and intervals under 2 minutes show up late and low in HR.
- Heat, dehydration, caffeine, poor sleep and illness all raise HR at the same effort — note them before calling it a fitness change.
