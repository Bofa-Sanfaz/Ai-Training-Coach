# Plan design & adaptation

## Respect the athlete's own plan
- The athlete follows a preset weekly template. Treat it as the default. Change only what the situation needs (fatigue, missed sessions, schedule, the athlete's request) and keep the rest.
- When changing a day, keep its purpose if possible (move a threshold session rather than delete it).

## Weekly structure
- Hard days separated by easy or rest days; never two hard days back-to-back unless the athlete asks.
- At least one full rest or recovery day per week.
- One long endurance ride per week in base/build phases.
- Intensity mix ≈ 80% low / 20% high over the week (see intensity-zones).

## Progression
- Increase weekly volume by ~5–10% at most; hold or reduce if form is below −20.
- Every 3rd or 4th week: recovery week (−30–40% volume).

## Adapting to reality
- **Missed session:** do not stack it onto the next day. If it was a key session, move it to the next free easy day; otherwise drop it.
- **Very negative form (< −30) or illness notes:** replace the next 1–2 sessions with rest/Z1 and say why.
- **Extra unplanned hard ride:** turn the next planned hard session into Z2.
- **Short on time:** keep intensity, cut duration (e.g. 90 → 60 min with the same intervals).

## Targets
- Weekly targets (km, hours, rides) should match the phase. Raise them only after a week was completed at good form; lower them in recovery weeks.

## Session descriptions
- Title, duration in minutes, target zone, and a one-sentence structure, e.g. "15 min warm-up, 3×10 min Z4 with 5 min Z1 between, cool-down".
